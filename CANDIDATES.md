## Honest assessment

1. The CTO gets caught by his own instructions, but that is a familiar workplace reversal. The timestamp adds texture rather than a strong final punch; the speaker could also be a human developer.
2. Clearly about an agent, but mostly an observation about an unreasonable human. Remembering tabs is less intuitive than keeping them open.
3. Ineligible: overlaps the explicitly excluded context-window/mid-sentence angle. The crying developer is an asserted reaction rather than a precise payoff.
4. Ineligible: the tiny-tweak premise overlaps the excluded small-change angle. The ticket close is neat, but the excessive rewrite is familiar.
5. Best balance: an unmistakable AI agent, an executive victim, a literal interpretation with a real consequence, and a dry ticket resolution. The boss-as-bottleneck premise is familiar, so freshness is 4 rather than 5. The founder phrase earns its position by turning the CEO into a disabled software feature.
6. Clean and short, but quarantine is a label rather than a consequential action; a human assistant could tell it too.
7. A strong runner-up: automating apologies makes bugs cheaper to ship. Continuous-remorse wordplay is a little more conspicuous than the winner's deadpan ending, and the narrator is not explicitly an AI agent.

## Selection and one further refinement

Choose #5. Replace the indirect opening with a direct instruction, identify the narrator as the agent, and split the action and ticket resolution into short beats. Preserve the final three-word punchline.

Final line:

> The CEO told me, his AI agent, to eliminate single points of failure. I revoked his admin access. Ticket closed: 'Founder mode disabled.'
